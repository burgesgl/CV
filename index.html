Research Focus

Closing the gap between policy design and classroom reality.

The Question

Why do teacher evaluation systems designed to support professional growth get implemented as compliance and punishment — and what conditions allow peer-based, developmental models to succeed instead?

The Angle

Music education has sustained peer-based adjudication and mentorship models (MPA, All-State, NCMEA) for nearly a century. These offer an instructive case for general education reform efforts that have stalled since Race to the Top.

The Methods

Comparative case study. Mixed methods. Interviews with music educators, administrators, and union officials. Secondary analysis of state policy documents and prior evaluation research (Donaldson & Woulfin, Kraft & Gilmour, Bleiberg et al.).

Scholarly Writing & Research

Writing Sample · 2025

Burgess, G. L.

Creating the Conditions for Effective Teacher Evaluation: Lessons from Music Education and State Reform

A comparative analysis of peer-based evaluation in music education (NCMEA's Music Performance Adjudication) and Tennessee's Core Coaching and Instructional Partnership Initiative. Argues that the 2010s evaluation reform movement's failure reflects a category error: treating teaching evaluation as a bureaucratic accountability problem rather than an epistemological challenge of expert judgment. Proposes a comparative case study synthesizing two functional peer-based systems into an implementable hybrid model.

Submitted as writing sample for UConn Neag School of Education PhD application, Learning, Leadership & Education Policy. References Donaldson & Woulfin (2018), Kraft & Gilmour (2017), Bleiberg et al. (2024), DeMitchell & Cobb (2006), Olson (2018).

Master's Thesis · 2021

Burgess, G. L.
University of Cincinnati,
College-Conservatory of Music
Advisor: Dr. Ann Porter

Timbral Discrimination of Preservice Music Education Students

An IRB-approved study measuring timbral discrimination abilities in preservice music education students (N=30) across 23 wind and orchestral instruments. Synthesizing research from psychoacoustics, neuroscience, and machine learning, participants identified instrument timbres under both unaltered (attack-sustain-release) and altered (attack removed) conditions. Overall labeling accuracy was 57.5% — comparable to machine performance but below the hypothesized 85% threshold. Secondary instrumental coursework showed significant positive correlation with accuracy (r = .48); aural skills coursework showed none (r = .03). Findings suggest systematic development of timbral discrimination within preparation curricula may address an underdeveloped component of pedagogical content knowledge.

Quantitative methods: correlation analysis, confusion matrices. Carries implications for teacher preparation policy and certification standards.

Signature Work · DAWs & Music Technology

Digital Audio Workstations as Gateways to Equity, CS, and Creative Expression

STEM Middle Academy · Springfield Public Schools · 2022–present

A sustained body of curriculum design, classroom implementation, and professional development work positioning DAWs as a vehicle for interdisciplinary learning at the intersection of music, computer science, and social justice. Core argument: in a district where 25.5% of families live below the poverty line and 75.9% have home internet access, free cloud-based production tools (BandLab, EarSketch) democratize music creation and build market-ready digital skills without cost or hardware barriers.

BandLab for Education

Primary DAW — cloud-based, Chromebook-compatible. Drag-and-drop composition, loops, sampling, MIDI, and audio file manipulation. Used for original composition projects including an ethnographic podcast unit ("What's in a Name?") and a culminating protest song project.

EarSketch (GA Tech × Pharrell Williams / YELLOW)

Code-based DAW using Python or JavaScript. Teaches computational thinking — loops, conditionals, custom functions — through music production. Students participate in the Your Voice is Power national remix competition, creating tracks that express a message promoting societal equity.

Chrome Music Lab

Introductory tool for music concepts and experimentation. Used in the limited-computer station-rotation model alongside unplugged rhythm composition, melody sketching, and lyric writing workshops.

The station-rotation model — DAW practice, song analysis, and unplugged composition in parallel — was designed specifically to ensure meaningful instruction regardless of computer availability. Equity of access to the creative process, not just the technology, is the design goal.
